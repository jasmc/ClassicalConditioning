# Independent scientific critique of the F/G/H review analysis

Audit date: 8 October 2026. Verdict: the fresh renderer satisfies the 0.5 s display contract, but I would not approve the present pipeline as a general time-resolved activity heatmap. It is a time- and sample-weighted projection of bout summaries conditional on detected movement, followed by trial-specific centring and, for C/D, contrast rescaling. Those choices need scientific justification separately from a correct rectangular grid.

The previous work established reproducibility, baseline centring and cell geometry. It did not establish that the chosen scalar answers the intended biological question. Calling it simply binned vigor conceals several consequential transformations.

## What the bout-median sentence actually means

The code first identifies eligible samples in the visible [-20,20) s trial window. It groups these samples by detected bout ID, computes one median of natural-log raw angular speed per bout, and copies that number back to every eligible sample of that bout. This occurs before 0.5 s aggregation.

For bout j, let `q_j = median(ln(v_i))` over its eligible visible samples. For half-second bin k, let `n_jk` be the number of eligible observations of that bout inside the bin. The resulting scalar is

`b_k = sum_j(n_jk*q_j) / sum_j(n_jk)`.

This is not the median of raw values within the bin, not their arithmetic mean, and not a mean of one value per bout. A bout occupying several bins carries the same q_j into all those bins. Its acceleration/deceleration and tail-beat peaks are discarded. Contributions are weighted by eligible time because cadence is fixed.

There are two separate problems:

1. **Nonlocal time dependence:** a bin's value may include intensity measurements outside that bin, possibly across CS or US boundaries. Calling its cell 0.5 s wide does not make its underlying signal local to those 0.5 s.
2. **Dependence on the plotting crop:** q_j is calculated after selecting the visible trial window, so changing +/-20 s to another plotting interval can alter the statistic for a boundary-crossing bout. A display crop should generally select which measurements to show, not define a biological event's intensity.

Concrete source example: F trial 27 contains a bout from -0.0402109375 to +0.2843110352 s. Its 29 pre-CS eligible samples have median log vigor -3.5910226184; its 200 post-CS samples have median -2.2958982420. The visible-bout median is -2.4383084615, which the code assigns to the pre-CS samples as well. The last baseline bin [-0.5,0) receives -2.4383084615, while a direct mean of its eligible log samples is -3.6159578670. After each candidate's own equal-bin-weight baseline median subtraction, those values become -0.0950526760 and -0.8792882137. This is observed temporal mixing, not a hypothetical concern. It does not prove the assigned higher value was caused by the CS; it proves post-CS observations participate in a pre-CS displayed scalar.

Measured onset-crossing bouts: F 38, G 43, H 13. Visible detected bout fragments span median 0.132, 0.110 and 0.235 s, respectively; maxima are 2.952, 3.281 and 2.524 s. Thus the summaries can span several cells. Compared with medians computed using the loaded 44 s detector neighbourhood, cropping changes 75 F bins, 90 G bins and 41 H bins. The context comparison is not certified to contain every complete whole-recording bout. It demonstrates crop dependence without claiming an exact complete-bout alternative.

Bout-level summaries are valid when the target is an event-level characteristic. For example, published zebrafish motor-kinematics work explicitly computes per-bout peak angular velocity and an early-bout integral. That does not validate assigning a whole-bout median to all time bins it overlaps. [Primary motor-kinematics study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.07.066).

## Assessment of each stage

**Stored angles and raw metric.** The code uses preserved local-angle values without adding the historical spatial filter, 700 FPS interpolation or temporal angle mean. Summing local angles and taking an absolute wrapped temporal derivative is the selected tail bend angular-speed definition. That is a legitimate defined metric, not a measure of whole-tail energy, distance or all segment motion; opposing local changes can cancel. I would retain the author's selected frame metric while examining transformations downstream. The derivative is sensitive to tracking jitter at approximately 1.423 ms intervals. Detector smoothing does not itself smooth the raw magnitude used for colour.

**Clock.** Fixed-cadence reconstruction is a plausible repair for buffered arrival timing and avoids dividing speed by irregular delivery intervals. FrameID gaps are respected. The epoch remains a camera arrival timestamp at a stable reference, so hardware exposure/onset latency is not independently measured. The current code appropriately calls this inferred acquisition timing. It is a reasonable review choice, not an authenticated millisecond stimulus synchronization. No evidence from this audit makes timing the main problem at 0.5 s resolution.

**Detector.** The 7/21/401-sample centred operations and amplitude/duration/gap rules decide which movements count. The rolling minimum's support, combined with median smoothing, can reach about 289 ms into the future. This is a support bound, not proof that every onset is shifted by 289 ms. It means eligibility around an onset can depend on later observations, separately from the whole-bout summary issue. Detection must be assessed against raw traces or independently annotated bouts, particularly around stimulus artifacts, weak movements and changes in bout form. Historical parameter inheritance alone does not validate the masks for these fish.

**Eligibility and missingness.** Only detector-valid, moving, positive raw observations contribute. Real low activity outside detected bouts is removed. A bin with no detected eligible samples has no scalar; it is not represented by a low-activity value. All 4,724 empty raw bins have complete detector support: F 754, G 698, H 3,272. None are completely unsupported detector bins. Thus black primarily denotes absence of detected eligible movement in these panels, despite being NaN numerically. H trial 16's C/D row adds a different meaning: an undefined normalization of otherwise observed values. The current display merges these meanings into black. They should be distinguished or explicitly described. No detector support threshold or zero filling is introduced by this audit.

**Natural logarithm.** Logs compress positive amplitudes and can be appropriate for multiplicative intensity variation. They also change the target: a mean of logs corresponds to a geometric summary, not mean raw activity. This is defensible only with a clear name and interpretation. Excluding zero/nonmoving observations to enable log calculation is a biological conditioning choice, not merely handling an inconvenient number. Changing rad/ms to deg/ms adds a constant to log values, which cancels after the present baseline subtraction and quantile-width calculations; the final centred values are not arbitrarily unit-dependent.

**Bout substitution.** This is the least well-justified stage for a time-resolved heatmap. It changes local intensity, erases within-bout dynamics, shares measurements across time bins and makes edge-event summaries depend on the visible crop. It should not remain simply because it was inherited from previous versions. Calculating complete-bout summaries before cropping would repair crop dependence but would preserve nonlocal mixing across stimulus boundaries.

**Bin scalar.** Finite-sample averaging and exact half-open bin edges are implemented correctly. Weighting by eligible sample count equals weighting by the eligible duration of bout contributions under constant cadence; it is neither automatically wrong nor a neutral default. A 10 ms contribution and a 400 ms contribution with the same q_j produce the same b_k when each is the sole contributor: the fraction of active time is absent from the colour. Among finite bins, median eligible fractions are 43.6% F, 49.9% G and 35.6% H. There are 77/70/133 bins with ten or fewer eligible samples, and a bin may have only one. Filling the entire cell is required by the 0.5 s display contract, but it conveys no support reliability on its own.

**Baseline median.** The new code correctly takes one vote per finite scalar baseline bin in [-15,0), after binning. This meets the explicit instruction and is robust to extreme scalar values. It is the baseline of supported movement-conditioned intensities, not the baseline activity over all 15 seconds. Because one bout can contribute to multiple bins, baseline scalars are not necessarily independent. Finite baseline-bin counts are F 19-30 (median 29), G 16-30 (median 28), H 1-25 (median 16). H has 13 trials with fewer than ten finite baseline bins. Those are descriptive audit counts, not a newly imposed ten-bin eligibility rule. One finite bin defines a median mathematically but supplies weak information about typical spontaneous behavior. Trial centring removes each trial's baseline level; it can therefore hide between-trial changes in absolute spontaneous vigor and should not itself be interpreted as learning.

**C/D scaling.** Both formulas act on the bin scalars and are mathematically centred. C divides by half the baseline P10-P90 width. D divides by the larger distance from baseline median to either percentile. Since `C_scale = (a+b)/2` and `D_scale = max(a,b)` for nonnegative `a=m-P10`, `b=P90-m`, D is always at least as large and at most twice C. D is consequently less aggressive. Neither is a z-score, fold change or significance score. The same normalized colour can represent different absolute intensity differences across trials/fish. F/G positive C scales vary approximately 4.9/4.4-fold within each fish; H scales vary 13.9-fold among defined trials. Control trial 65 has C scale 0.0225975, so a small log-intensity difference can reach an endpoint. This is amplified contrast, not evidence of a large absolute effect.

**Clipping and colours.** Among finite values, C puts 29.7% F, 28.8% G and 30.8% H exactly at an endpoint; D puts 19.2%, 15.2% and 21.5% there. Clipping erases magnitude differences beyond those endpoints. It occurs even in baseline, so endpoint colours are not a biological response threshold. Keep unclipped numbers, report saturation and use C/D as exploratory relative-contrast views. The palette and zero-midpoint mapping are correct. A zero median does not require symmetric hue saturation, equal brightness or identical visual areas of blue/yellow. Such properties cannot be used as scientific validation.

**Scope of inference.** Three selected fish are descriptive examples. Correct cells, zero medians and a compelling visual pattern cannot demonstrate a population learning effect, suppression, extinction or precise response latency. No such inference was estimated in this build.

## The target differs from the author's metric-selection evidence

The frozen metric-selection document explicitly describes zero-inclusive total activity, rather than moving-only intensity. The current figure starts with the same angular-speed formula but applies detector conditioning, logs and event substitution, so it has a different final measurement target. One can retain the same raw metric and still change what biological quantity is being shown. [Local metric-selection decision](../../../docs/analysis/METRIC_SELECTION_2026-10-06.md).

This distinction also appears in primary conditioning literature: Aizenberg and Schuman define activity over an interval as the sum of absolute tail angular velocities, then compare CS and baseline activity. This is a precedent for total activity, not a mandate to copy their preprocessing or assay. [Primary conditioning study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6622926/).

## Sensitivity evidence and recommended direction

Replacing bout summaries with direct eligible frame log values, then taking the same half-second mean and each candidate's own post-bin baseline median, changes the side of baseline in 970/6,446 F bins, 827/6,502 G bins and 1,135/3,928 H bins (15.0%, 12.7%, 28.9%). Close-to-zero signs can be sensitive to small numerical/statistical differences; even requiring both magnitudes to exceed 0.05 log units leaves 325/209/301 opposite-sign cells. Mean absolute centred-bin differences are 0.112/0.104/0.139 log units. This demonstrates material sensitivity; it does not certify the direct-log alternative as the uniquely correct target.

For an intensity-among-detected-movement heatmap, the smallest justified simplification is to remove bout-value substitution and directly summarize eligible samples local to each half-second bin. Define mean or median explicitly; show detector occupancy/support separately; preserve the requested post-bin baseline and midpoint. This preserves the detector-conditioned target but avoids painting future parts of a bout into earlier bin values. The detector's own centred support remains a separate timing issue.

For a heatmap intended to show the amount of activity associated with conditioning, I would instead start from the selected raw angular speed and calculate mean speed over all valid observed frames in each half-second interval, or its time integral. Include observed low speeds and true zeros; retain invalid frames as missing. At fixed bin duration and good coverage, mean speed and the time integral are proportional. This target retains both intensity and time spent moving and aligns more closely with the selected total-activity analysis. If log compression is wanted, specify it after choosing the raw bin statistic and address true zero bins explicitly; do not silently add an arbitrary pseudocount. A detected-bout occupancy panel can complement it.

I would establish that target before selecting a colour range. The centred log reference on a common scale is easier to compare across trials than per-trial C/D, although its +/-0.25 range is also arbitrary and clips visually. I would keep C/D as named exploratory views and avoid choosing the final normalization solely for appearance. No new scientific threshold, upstream edit or replacement figure is made in this audit.

## Reproduction and limitations

`audit.py` rehashes all figure source inputs, reconstructs raw speed and the detector with the existing functions, and verifies agreement with all 21,600 delivered uncentred scalars before computing diagnostics. It saves per-bin, per-bout, per-trial and boundary-crossing records plus `outputs/summary.json`. The diagnostic target is the figure transformation, not a new independently hand-annotated bout detector. It does not comprehensively validate tracking, biological event classification, whole-recording event boundaries, protocol hardware latency or inference. Arithmetic disagreement between candidates is evidence of different measurements, not proof of superiority.

All figure files, shared preprocessing and prior historical outputs are preserved.
