## Bout/no-bout periods and Figure 4

The author asks how an individual LME behaves when periods with bouts are followed by periods without bouts, analogous to unavailable intensity trials in Figure 2's third row, and how this affects pooled learners versus nonlearners in Figure 4.

### Establish what is observed

1. Distinguish technically valid tracking with no detected bout, valid detected bouts with measurable intensity, invalid/unavailable tracking, and windows with insufficient observation coverage. No detected bout is a behavioral observation under the detector, not proof of complete physical immobility.
2. For conditional bout intensity, a valid no-bout window has undefined intensity. For movement occurrence or observed fraction moving, valid silence contributes absence/zero under that outcome's definition. Inadequately observed tracking remains unavailable, not zero.
3. A missing baseline or response intensity makes the paired G difference unavailable. An LME can use unequal numbers of available measurements, but cannot infer an observed silent-window bout intensity. Complete absence of observations in a fish/epoch makes any pooled prediction especially model-dependent.
4. Quantify availability and no-bout frequencies by fish, trial, phase, condition and candidate score/stratum. Suppression may change which intensity values are observable; standard missing-data assumptions are therefore a scientific issue.

### Compare suitable measurement/model routes

Retain the existing conditional-intensity analysis with its restricted interpretation and separate contribution counts. Compare a two-part description or model only if it addresses the observed data: movement/bout occurrence (or a justified duration-based measure) plus intensity conditional on movement. A joint model may be considered if dependence between occurrence and intensity materially affects inference.

Also assess the established zero-inclusive total-activity/profile route under Plan 05. Compute it from its actual approved raw observation/aggregation definition. Do not recover it by inserting zeros into log intensity, or assume that occurrence multiplied by a median bout intensity equals mean total activity.

At both population and individual levels, test whether apparent weak acquisition or nonclassification results from lost conditional-intensity observations during valid silence. Do not automatically exclude those fish or turn no-bout intensity into a learner/nonlearner decision.

### Carry the distinction into pooled learner/nonlearner profiles

1. Preserve the current Plan 05 estimand and technical-coverage policy until a scientific change is selected. Confirm how valid no-bout time contributes to scaled total activity; invalid tracking must not dilute the denominator.
2. Aggregate trials/time bins within each fish before summarizing across fish. Keep equal fish weight; concatenating all bouts would overweight fish with more bouts and could remove silent fish from the apparent group.
3. Report stratum membership counts separately from outcome contributors at each time bin, with trial counts, valid time and no-bout fractions. Use the whole fish as the population uncertainty unit.
4. Show how learner scores/labels and group differences change when no-bout information is represented appropriately, without changing the primary technical cohort.
5. Keep same-data learner/nonlearner contrasts descriptive. Inference requires a justified held-out, cross-fitted or independent design accounting for score construction, threshold/reference fitting and selection. Reusing the defining suppression outcome is not independent confirmation.
6. If the chosen Figure 3 representation is continuous, adapt Figure 4 to that representation rather than inventing binary strata.

**Bout-availability deliverable:** A bout-availability/estimand audit and a proposed Figure 4 comparison design under Plan 05, explaining whether conditional intensity, occurrence, total activity or their combination supports each claim.

## Individual and population response-profile models

### Author clarification and purpose

The author clarified that the proposal includes estimating a time-resolved profile for each fish despite unavailable bout-intensity bins, then combining those fitted profiles within learner/nonlearner groups. It also includes fitting population models separately for learners, nonlearners, controls and any other scientifically approved strata. The author subsequently requested explicit consideration of a GAMM. These are candidate estimation strategies to compare before selecting the Figure 4 analysis.

A single hierarchical model can provide both group curves and fish-specific predictions. Distinguish this from independently fitting a separate regression to every fish: the latter shares no information across fish and may be unstable for sparse records. A model containing only one fish cannot estimate between-fish random-effect variation, although that fish's repeated trials may support other levels of variation.

### Target, input and time coordinates

1. Define the Figure 4 time axis as seconds relative to the CS, with block/phase and original trial number retained as additional coordinates where needed. Figure 2's third row models change over scheduled trial number; its fitted curve cannot simply substitute for the within-trial Figure 4 profile.
2. Authenticate the actual profile inputs before fitting. The [supported Figure 4 reference](../docs/running-analysis/figure4.md#signal-and-provenance) describes signed bout-log-vigor bins with no-bout intensity unavailable and movement probability separate; parent Plan 05 also describes scaled total activity. Reconcile the selected target and source identity rather than treating those quantities as interchangeable or silently changing the outcome.
3. Fit authenticated trial/fish-level observations or explicitly justified fish summaries, retaining their dependence and contribution information. Fitting only the displayed pooled group median discards information needed for the proposed hierarchy and individual predictions.
4. Specify whether the target is conditional bout intensity, occurrence, an overall activity measure, or separately reported components. Name the target as a mean, median or other quantity. A fitted conditional mean is not the existing across-fish median.
5. Keep scientific group definitions and technical cohort membership separate. Categories remain conditional on the Figure 3 representation review; retain a continuous-score alternative when categories are not justified.

### Candidate estimation strategies

| Strategy | Individual output | Group output and comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Observed fish-first profiles | Existing observations and within-fish summaries | Preserve the authenticated descriptive reference and per-bin contributors |
| Independent per-fish regressions followed by pooling | Each fish is fitted independently | Equal-fish summary of fitted curves; simple benchmark only where individual support is adequate |
| Hierarchical fish-specific curves followed by pooling | Fixed-plus-random predictions from a shared mixed model | Equal-fish summaries with uncertainty from the complete fitting/summarizing procedure |
| Separate population mixed models by stratum | Fish effects are estimated within each stratum | One learner model, one nonlearner model, control models as appropriate; compare their curves using a declared joint uncertainty procedure |
| One joint mixed model with group-specific curves | Group profile plus individual deviations | Direct group contrasts and individual profiles in one framework; allow justified group-specific variance or dependence |

Use a joint model with group-specific profiles as the initial candidate, with separate stratum models as a sensitivity comparison. This is a proposed comparison order, not selection of the final method. Separate fits and pooled individual predictions need not yield the same curves: weighting, smoothing, covariance sharing, estimands and partial pooling can differ. Identify equivalent specifications where they exist and explain material disagreements.

Do not force groups to share a response shape merely because they are fitted together. Include group-by-time structure and block/phase interactions where justified, with fish effects and appropriately nested trial variation or residual dependence. Compare only a bounded set supported by the available fish and trials. Define any cross-assay model explicitly; shared controls must not become independent duplicated fish.

### Where a GAMM makes sense

Include a penalized GAMM as a candidate extension of the hierarchical and population strategies above. Group-specific smooth functions of CS-relative time can represent curved, delayed or multi-peaked responses; fish-specific smooth deviations may be useful if repeated observations support them. Block/phase differences may require separate or interacting terms. A spline LME also permits nonlinear profiles, so the comparison concerns the basis, penalty, hierarchy and uncertainty rather than a straight line versus a curve.

1. Start with a simple, bounded spline LME and a penalized GAMM on comparable targets and inputs. Assess estimated smoothness, basis adequacy, residual dependence, variance differences and heavy tails. Add fish-specific smooths only when they improve supported individual estimation or prediction.
2. Use an observation family appropriate to the quantity: occurrence, fractions, counts and signed intensity are distinct outcomes. A GAMM does not require a Gamma response distribution. Consider the previously proposed two-part mixed model when occurrence and conditional intensity are both needed; do not assume one Gaussian intensity smoother describes the entire behavior.
3. Check CS/US events and actual protocol timing. Do not smooth automatically across a genuine discontinuity, an unsupported long gap or an assay boundary. Compare event-aware specifications, and avoid forcing equal derivatives or one common smoothness level without justification.
4. Assess whether smoothing displaces response peaks, creates activity between separated bout periods, or gives narrow bands in regions with little information. Numerical convergence and attractive curves do not establish adequate calibration.
5. Retain the simpler LME or observed reference if the GAMM adds no stable improvement in calibration, prediction or scientific interpretation. Demote an unsupported GAMM and document why; it remains an evaluated proposal, not a mandatory model.

### Predictions, pooling and uncertainty

1. Evaluate candidate curves on a common CS-aligned grid within declared support, preserving block, phase, catches and actual event identities. Mark whether each region is directly supported, interpolated, or extrapolated; define how much unsupported prediction is acceptable before inspecting comparisons.
2. A fitted curve can predict an expected conditional bout-intensity value where no intensity measurement exists. This is a model-based prediction under its assumptions, not an observed bout or reconstructed actual activity during a valid quiet interval. Keep observed data intact and show occurrence/availability alongside the intensity predictions.
3. Summarize fitted profiles with the declared equal-fish target. Distinguish predictions averaged over enrolled fish from a reference-fish fixed-effect curve and a curve averaged over a population random-effects distribution. These can differ, especially with nonlinear links; specify which each displayed line represents.
4. Propagate individual-estimation, shared fixed-effect, random-effect/covariance and smoothing uncertainty as applicable. Fitted fish curves share estimated model parameters; their point estimates are not independent, error-free observations for a second-stage group test.
5. Prefer contrasts from the joint model or an appropriately validated resampling procedure refitting the full pipeline. When comparing separate group models, refit all relevant models within each uncertainty replicate. Preserve each fish's repeated observations and shared-control identity; fish are the population resampling units.
6. State whether group uncertainty is conditional on fixed learner labels or includes their estimation/selection uncertainty. Where appropriate, repeat score/reference/threshold construction inside the validation procedure. This alone does not make same-data learner differences independent validation.
7. Distinguish fish variation, uncertainty in an expected group curve and prediction intervals for future observations. Define pointwise bands versus simultaneous contrast bands and their time/group/block family. Compare group differences directly rather than judging overlap of separately plotted bands.
8. A label-informed model can shrink sparse fish toward their assigned group's curve. Display individual support and assess label-blind fits as a sensitivity; the resulting individual profiles must not become independent evidence for those same labels.

### Validation and decision sequence

1. Audit profile sources, observation support, existing group/score identities and the exact conditional versus overall target. Preserve the current observed profiles as the descriptive comparator.
2. Compare a bounded set of simple LME and GAMM specifications across the strategies above on identical inputs. Save individual predictions, group curves, contrasts, contributor/support masks and failure reasons, with source/configuration hashes.
3. Evaluate available-data prediction using honest trial/fish or contiguous-time holdouts appropriate to the target. Masking observed bout bins can test conditional-intensity prediction; it cannot directly validate intensity where no bout occurred. Keep occurrence prediction and total-activity evaluation separate. Previously inspected data do not acquire prospective holdout status.
4. Simulate known individual/group profiles with rapid and gradual responses, multiple peaks, stimulus-boundary changes, long quiet intervals, technical gaps, serial dependence, sparse groups and heterogeneous fish. Assess curve/contrast coverage, bias, peak timing, failure rates and sensitivity to smoothing and group construction. Report Monte Carlo uncertainty and retain failed cases.
5. Compare observed and fitted Figure 4 profiles side by side in a separate review, identifying unsupported regions and target differences. Choose by calibration, predictive performance, stability and interpretation; smoothness or stronger learner/nonlearner separation alone is insufficient.
6. Keep same-data group comparisons descriptive unless an adequate validation design supports inference. If Figure 3 selects continuous evidence, model its relationship with response profiles without inventing learner categories.
7. Record the selected approach, target, permitted contrasts and limitations under the parent statistical/learner/profile plans before preparing a replacement figure. This work requests no new analysis execution, current-state publication or freeze.

## CR characterization, response magnitude and peak timing

**Author addition, 2026-10-22:** Use the proposed individual and population profile models to characterize the conditioned response statistically, including when it reaches its maximum, and compare learners with matched controls and nonlearners. This extends the planned Figure 4 comparison; it does not supply approved learner classes, numerical peak criteria or a completed result.

### Scientific targets and response direction

1. Define the CR outcome and sign before extracting a peak. For a suppression CR, the strongest response is a reduction in the selected intensity, movement or activity quantity; it is not necessarily the maximum raw vigor. Choose a direction-oriented response so that larger values consistently mean stronger conditioned expression.
2. Distinguish a group's suppression relative to its own declared baseline from its separation from matched controls. For example, a control-relative suppression curve can be defined as the control expected response minus the learner expected response at the same CS-relative time, block and assay, on the same approved scale. Its maximum is the time of greatest group separation. This need not coincide with the learner group's own response trough if controls also change over time. Show the underlying group curves.
3. Define an assay-specific CR search window before evaluating candidate peaks, using authenticated stimulus timing. Separate anticipatory pre-US responses from the unconditioned response following actual US onset. For catch/Test trials, use the verified paired-training expected-US reference where appropriate. Do not select a wider window after inspecting a favorable extremum.
4. Retain seconds from CS onset and, if the scientific comparison requires it, latency relative to verified expected US onset. Cross-assay comparisons must retain the different schedules and response windows; time normalization is a separate explicit scientific choice.
5. Separate the peak of a fitted population curve from the distribution of individual fish peak times. Averaging fish profiles and then finding the peak generally answers a different question from averaging or comparing their individual peak times. Report which is estimated and retain individual prediction uncertainty.

| Candidate feature | Estimand to define before execution |
|---|---|
| Full CR shape | Group response or group-difference curve throughout the declared CR window |
| Maximum CR magnitude | Largest direction-oriented response within that window, with its baseline/control reference stated |
| Peak latency | Time attaining that maximum, or a supported peak region when one time cannot be identified |
| Integrated response | Signed area or another explicitly defined summary over a fixed window; do not choose signed/positive-part aggregation after seeing results |
| Response duration and within-trial onset/offset | Optional threshold-and-persistence features with justified criteria and uncertainty; distinct from acquisition across training trials |

Select primary and secondary features and the comparison family before final analysis. The strongest point on a conditional bout-intensity curve describes conditional intensity. If bouts disappear, evaluate occurrence/overall activity separately rather than interpreting an interpolated conditional-intensity trough as observed maximal behavioral suppression.

### Planned statistical comparisons

1. Prioritize learner–control and learner–nonlearner profile comparisons within each assay, with control–nonlearner or cross-assay comparisons included only for a defined scientific question. Condition identity and any control-classifier strata remain explicit; controls are not automatically biologically equivalent to the nonlearner group.
2. Use the joint group-specific LME/GAMM candidate for direct contrasts, with the separate-group and pooled-individual approaches as declared comparisons. Allow justified group-by-time and block/phase differences while preserving the fish/trial hierarchy.
3. Estimate the full difference curve and its simultaneous uncertainty over the declared time/group/block family. A group being individually significant while another is not does not establish a group difference; compare the relevant curves or derived quantities directly.
4. Distinguish differences between each group's own peak amplitudes/times from differences evaluated at a common prespecified time. The maximum of a difference curve is another target. These quantities must not be substituted for one another to obtain a stronger result.
5. For a control-relative target, propagate uncertainty in the common control/reference curve and its covariance with all comparisons. Shared controls across assays retain their original acquisition identities in fitting and resampling.
6. Report effect estimates and uncertainty on the declared scientific scale. Reserve confirmatory group inference for a justified validation design. Learner labels constructed from the same response data can create apparent differences in magnitude and shape; a new time/peak summary is not automatically independent evidence of learning.
7. If Figure 3 retains continuous scores, characterize how profile shape and peak features vary with that score without inventing a binary split. The same-data and prediction-support limits still apply.

### Estimating and validating peak uncertainty

1. Derive extrema from the complete fitted response or contrast on the selected scientific scale, including relevant parametric group effects and reference terms. Do not find peaks of an isolated centered smooth when the claim concerns a whole-model response.
2. Compare a calibrated whole-fish refit procedure with joint coefficient/posterior simulation where appropriate. Recompute the complete curve, reference, extremum and timing in every accepted replicate/draw, accounting for shared parameters and the chosen treatment of label uncertainty. Preserve failed fits and cases with unsupported/no localized peaks.
3. A standard error for the curve at the selected extremum is not uncertainty for a peak selected by searching over time. Validate the peak-amplitude and peak-latency procedure itself, including the effects of searching, smoothing and covariance estimation. Distinguish approximate posterior intervals from calibrated frequentist confidence intervals.
4. Derivatives can help assess rising/falling portions of a supported curve, but a derivative interval containing zero does not uniquely identify a peak or provide a peak-time interval. Apply relevant whole-response derivatives and simultaneous uncertainty when making interval-wide claims.
5. Handle broad plateaus, near-tied peaks, flat/no-response curves, boundary extrema and peaks in unsupported gaps explicitly. A fitted curve always has an extremum on a closed grid; this alone does not establish a meaningful conditioned-response peak. Report a peak region, multiple plausible regions, an unresolved peak, or a window-limited result rather than forcing a precise time. Any peak-region tolerance needs scientific justification.
6. Retain ambiguity in bootstrap/posterior summaries rather than dropping draws without a unique or meaningful peak. Examine multimodal timing distributions and avoid a misleading single symmetric interval. Match reported timing precision to binning, event timing, model support and validated recovery.
7. Extend the planned simulations to known peak amplitudes/latencies, broad plateaus, multiple peaks, absent responses, moving control baselines, boundary peaks, quiet intervals, technical gaps and heterogeneous individual timing. Evaluate feature bias, uncertainty coverage, false peak/timing claims and sensitivity to basis/penalty choices. Include full pipelines when group construction is part of the inferential target.
8. If peak localization is unreliable, retain interpretable full curves or prespecified window averages and report the timing limitation. This does not justify selecting whichever method gives the earliest or sharpest peak.

**CR-characterization deliverable:** A proposal and subsequent validated comparison table for CR shape, magnitude and supported peak latency/regions, with direct learner–control and learner–nonlearner contrasts, underlying observed/fitted curves, contribution/support information, uncertainty, multiplicity and validation mode. Include individual timing distributions only where their estimation is adequately supported. Keep within-trial CR timing distinct from population acquisition onset and later extinction.

## Methodological references

- [nlme mixed-effects specification](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/nlme/help/lme.html): nested effects, residual correlation and unequal variance.
- [nlme population and individual predictions](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/nlme/html/predict.lme.html): fixed-effect predictions and predictions including estimated individual effects.
- [mgcv factor smooths](https://www.stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/factor.smooth.html): group-specific smooths and random individual curves.
- [Two-part repeated-measures model](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1191/0962280202sm291ra): occurrence and positive-amount components with correlated random effects; applying this structure to the selected fish outcomes requires its own observation-model review.
- [mgcv derived predictions](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/predict.gam.html): whole-model predictions and coefficient simulation for derived quantities; peak/timing calibration remains a separate task.
- [gratia derivatives](https://gavinsimpson.github.io/gratia/reference/derivatives.html): derivative estimates and pointwise/simultaneous intervals; derivative significance is not a peak-time confidence interval.
