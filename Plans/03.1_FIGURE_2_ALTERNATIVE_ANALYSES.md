### Specification and inference

1. Audit the actual cues, response windows, US timing, phase boundaries, recording gaps and context. Measuring before the first omitted US motivates continuity into the first Test trial when preceding cues/context are unchanged. It does not require identical observed values, equal derivatives, or one rigid smooth across phases.
2. Retain direct response-minus-baseline and baseline-adjusted-response alternatives with explicit estimands. Assess whether adjusting for a contemporaneous baseline changes the biological interpretation.
3. Bound smooth complexity and fish-level effects. Check within-fish residual dependence, heavy tails, heteroscedasticity and influence. Correlation must respect scheduled gaps; adjacent available rows need not be adjacent trials. Add further model families only when diagnostics identify a specific need.
4. Define separately the global equality null, differential-change null, planned block/phase averages and trial contrasts. One fitted GAMM can supply multiple contrasts; it does not automatically resolve their multiplicity.
5. Construct simultaneous uncertainty for the declared trial family from the complete contrast, including condition offsets and reference covariance. Account for smoothing/covariance estimation and failed refits as appropriate. Do not infer group differences from overlap of separate group bands.
6. Compare acquisition estimates from training-only fits with those from fits using Test observations, since later data can influence earlier smoothed estimates.
7. If timing is pursued, specify effect magnitude, persistence on the scheduled grid, missing-trial handling, and resamples with no detected onset before calculation. Distinguish first detectable from sustained divergence. A supported interval is not automatically an onset confidence interval.
8. Keep recovery/extinction separate. A later interval containing zero cannot establish equality or extinction.

### Validation and presentation decision

Compare curve and planned-average coverage, false detections, timing recovery, fit failures, influence and robustness at the actual fish/trial counts. Include null, pre-existing offsets, rapid/gradual acquisition, plateaus, transient effects, heavy tails, autocorrelation, unequal observation coverage, behavioral silence and technical gaps. Use sufficient independent simulations to report Monte Carlo uncertainty. Successful optimization or more bootstrap draws cannot repair mean-function bias.

Review a candidate G/H/I presentation with observed condition trajectories, an aligned Pre-referenced contrast and simultaneous band, phase guides and a compact supported-interval strip. Planned block effects may be summarized alongside or in supporting material. Keep descriptive medians and model estimates distinguishable. Replacing current statistical rows remains conditional on the comparison and author selection; failed timing calibration limits claims to supported coarser summaries.
